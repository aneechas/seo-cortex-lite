# EEAT Assessment Rubric

## Purpose

Framework for evaluating and improving Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness signals across a website. Based on Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines.

## EEAT Dimensions

### Experience (First-Hand Knowledge)

**Signals to assess:**
- Original photographs, screenshots, or media demonstrating real use
- Specific anecdotes or case studies from direct experience
- First-person accounts with verifiable detail
- Product/service reviews from actual users
- Before/after evidence, timestamps, real data

**How to improve:**
- Add original photos and screenshots (not stock images)
- Include case studies with specific results and timelines
- Share process documentation (how something was actually done)
- Feature real customer stories with permission
- Show proof of experience (certifications, project history)

**Score 0-5:** 0 = No experience signals. 5 = Abundant, verifiable first-hand evidence.

### Expertise (Knowledge and Skill)

**Signals to assess:**
- Author qualifications and credentials displayed
- Depth and accuracy of technical information
- Correct use of specialist terminology
- References to primary sources and research
- Content that demonstrates genuine understanding, not surface-level

**How to improve:**
- Add author bylines with relevant credentials
- Create author pages with professional background
- Ensure content depth matches or exceeds top-ranking competitors
- Reference primary sources (not just other blog posts)
- Have content reviewed by subject matter experts

**Score 0-5:** 0 = No expertise signals, generic content. 5 = Clear expert authorship, deep knowledge.

### Authoritativeness (Recognition by Others)

**Signals to assess:**
- Backlinks from authoritative, relevant sites
- Mentions and citations in industry publications
- Awards, recognition, industry associations
- Social proof (follower counts, engagement, press coverage)
- Featured in expert roundups or quoted as a source

**How to improve:**
- Build links from industry-relevant authority sites
- Seek press coverage and industry mentions
- Contribute to authoritative publications (guest articles)
- Join relevant industry associations and directories
- Build entity signals (Knowledge Panel, Wikipedia where warranted)

**Score 0-5:** 0 = No external recognition. 5 = Widely recognised authority in the field.

### Trustworthiness (Reliability and Honesty)

**Signals to assess:**
- HTTPS and security implementation
- Clear contact information and physical address
- Privacy policy, terms of service, editorial policy
- Transparent about who creates and reviews content
- Accurate, up-to-date information
- No deceptive practices (hidden costs, misleading claims)
- Customer reviews and ratings (genuine, not fabricated)

**How to improve:**
- Ensure HTTPS across entire site
- Provide clear, accessible contact information
- Publish editorial standards and content creation process
- Display genuine customer reviews
- Maintain accurate, regularly updated content
- Include editorial review dates on content
- Be transparent about commercial relationships (affiliate disclosures)

**Score 0-5:** 0 = Trust signals absent, potential red flags. 5 = Comprehensive trust signals, full transparency.

## YMYL Considerations

**Your Money or Your Life (YMYL) topics require higher EEAT standards:**
- Health and medical information
- Financial advice and services
- Legal information
- News and current events
- Safety information
- Government and civic information

For YMYL content: minimum EEAT score should be 15/20 across all dimensions. Below that, content may be suppressed in rankings.

## Overall EEAT Score

| Total Score | Rating | Interpretation |
|------------|--------|---------------|
| 17-20 | Excellent | Strong EEAT signals across all dimensions |
| 13-16 | Good | Solid foundation, targeted improvements possible |
| 9-12 | Adequate | Gaps in one or more dimensions need attention |
| 5-8 | Weak | Significant EEAT deficiencies affecting rankings |
| 0-4 | Critical | Fundamental EEAT problems, especially risky for YMYL |
